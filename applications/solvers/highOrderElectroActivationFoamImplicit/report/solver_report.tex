       \texorpdfstring{\texttt{highOrderElectroActivationFoamImplicit}}{highOrderElectroActivationFoamImplicit}\\
       \large A high-order, implicit Picard
       monodomain solver in OpenFOAM with an Eigen linear-algebra back-end}
\author{Pablo}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This report specifies the algorithms implemented in the OpenFOAM solver:

\begin{center}
\code{highOrderElectroActivationFoamImplicit}.
\end{center}

The solver advances the
monodomain equations of cardiac electrophysiology coupled to the
ten Tusscher--Noble--Noble--Panfilov (TNNP, 2004) ionic cell model. The
diffusion operator is discretised with the cell-centred finite-volume
framework of OpenFOAM, optionally upgraded to a Local Regression Estimators
(LRE) high-order reconstruction; the ionic source is evaluated either at
cell centres or at LRE cell quadrature points; time integration follows a
$\theta$-scheme (backward Euler or Crank--Nicolson); the resulting
\emph{nonlinear} coupled system is solved with a fixed-point Picard
iteration using a fixed number of nonlinear sweeps. In each sweep the
ionic ODE is integrated at the current frozen voltage, the ionic source
is placed on the right-hand side, and the voltage is updated by solving
the unchanged implicit diffusion operator. The sparse linear system is
factored or iteratively solved with Eigen
(\code{SparseLU} or \code{BiCGSTAB+ILUT}); the per-cell ionic ODE uses
OpenFOAM's adaptive RKF45 integrator with configurable tolerances; the
TNNP cell model is wrapped in a phase-aware \code{protectState} guard
that enforces physical bounds before every algebraic evaluation;
activation times are recorded by linear interpolation across the first
threshold crossing and benchmark points are sampled by inverse-distance
weighting. The report also discusses the limits of the single-sweep
Picard regime used in the reported runs (where the ionic source is
effectively lagged by one step and the temporal error is no longer
controlled by $\theta$) and the assumptions, scope, and side-effects of
the TNNP \code{protectState} guard, including its interaction with the
RKF45 step controller. All algorithmic parameters are exposed through the
\code{constant/spatialIntegrationProperties} and
\code{constant/timeIntegrationProperties} dictionaries, and the
companion Python driver \code{run\_convergence.py} generates these
dictionaries from a single configuration file. Section~\ref{sec:results}
collects measured P8 activation times, wall-clock cost and peak memory
from four independent result trees of a 2D Niederer 2012 benchmark.
\end{abstract}

% ================================================================== %
\section{Governing equations}
% ================================================================== %

\subsection{Monodomain PDE}

The bulk voltage is propagated by the standard monodomain
reaction--diffusion equation~\cite{ttp2004,niederer2012}:
\begin{equation}
  \chi \, C_{m} \, \frac{\partial \Vm}{\partial t}
   \;=\;
   \divg\!\bigl( \Dten \grad \Vm \bigr)
   - \Iion(\Vm, \mathbf{s})
   + \Iext
   \quad \text{in } \Omega \times (0, T],
   \label{eq:monodomain}
\end{equation}
subject to insulated boundaries
$\mathbf{n} \cdot ( \Dten \grad \Vm ) = 0$ on
$\partial \Omega \times (0, T]$, and initial condition
$\Vm(\mathbf{x}, 0) = -\SI{84}{\milli\volt}$. The conductivity tensor
$\Dten$ is symmetric positive-definite and either uniform or read as a
\code{volTensorField} from disk.

Dividing~\eqref{eq:monodomain} by $\chi C_{m}$ gives the form actually
used inside the solver:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \Vm}{\partial t}
   \;=\;
   \mathcal{L} \Vm
   \;+\;
   s(\Vm, \mathbf{s}),
   \qquad
   \mathcal{L} \;=\; \frac{1}{\chi C_{m}}\,\divg(\Dten \grad \cdot),
   \quad
   s \;=\; -\frac{\Iion}{\chi C_{m}} + \frac{\Iext}{\chi C_{m}}.
   \label{eq:semi-discrete}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Ionic ODE}

At every spatial sampling point the TNNP state vector
$\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^{17}$ evolves according to
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}}{\mathrm{d}t}
   \;=\;
   \mathbf{f}_{\text{TNNP}}\!\bigl(\Vm, \mathbf{s}; \texttt{tissue}\bigr),
   \qquad \mathbf{s}(0) = \mathbf{s}_{0},
   \label{eq:tnnp}
\end{equation}
where \code{tissue} selects \code{epicardialCells}, \code{mCells} or
\code{endocardialCells}. The 17 tracked states are
\[
  \mathbf{s} = (V,\ K_{i},\ Na_{i},\ Ca_{i},\ Xr_{1},\ Xr_{2},\ Xs,\ m,\ h,\ j,\ d,\ f,\ fCa,\ s,\ r,\ g,\ Ca_{SR}).
\]
The total ionic current returned to the PDE is \code{Iion\_cm} of
TNNP~\cite{ttp2004}.

\paragraph{Units and sign convention.} The field \code{Iion\_cm}
returned by the TNNP wrapper is the per-capacitance ionic current,
\begin{equation*}
  \code{Iion\_cm}
   \;=\;
   \frac{I_{\text{ion}}^{\text{(memb)}}}{C_{m}}
   \;\equiv\;
   \frac{1}{\chi C_{m}}\,
   \bigl(\chi\, I_{\text{ion}}^{\text{(memb)}}\bigr),
\end{equation*}
expressed in V/s, with the electrophysiological convention
\emph{outward (cation efflux) current positive}. Equation
\eqref{eq:semi-discrete} therefore adds $-\,\code{Iion\_cm}$ to the
PDE source; the external stimulus \code{externalStimulusCurrent} is
stored in A/m$^{3}$ and divided by $\chi C_{m}$ inside the solver
(see \code{createFields.H} and the source-vector assembly in the
main solver loop). Throughout the rest of this report the symbol
$\Iion$ is used as a shorthand for \code{Iion\_cm}; no further
rescaling is applied between the cell model and the linear system.

\subsection{External stimulus}

\begin{equation}
  \Iext(\mathbf{x}, t) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    I_{0} & \mathbf{x} \in \Omega_{\text{stim}}^{(b)},
            \;\; t \in [t_{0}^{(b)},\, t_{0}^{(b)} + \tau], \\
    0     & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with Niederer-benchmark defaults
$I_{0} = \SI{50000}{\ampere\per\meter\cubed}$ and
$\tau = \SI{2}{\milli\second}$.

% ================================================================== %
\section{Time discretisation: the $\theta$-scheme}
% ================================================================== %

Time is advanced from $t^{n}$ to $t^{n+1} = t^{n} + \Delta t$ by
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathsf{V}^{n+1} - \mathsf{V}^{n}}{\Delta t}
   \;=\;
   \theta\, \Lop\, \mathsf{V}^{n+1}
   + (1-\theta)\, \Lop\, \mathsf{V}^{n}
   + \theta\, \mathbf{s}^{n+1}
   + (1-\theta)\, \mathbf{s}^{n}.
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcl@{}}
\toprule
\code{implicitScheme} & $\theta$ & Scheme \\
\midrule
\code{backwardEuler} & $1$   & First-order, L-stable \\
\code{crankNicolson} & $1/2$ & Second-order, A-stable \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

After dividing by $\Delta t$ the implementation forms
\begin{equation}
  \bigl[\Mmass/\Delta t \,-\, \theta\, \Lop\bigr]\, \mathsf{V}^{n+1}
   \;=\;
   \bigl[\Mmass/\Delta t \,+\, (1-\theta)\, \Lop\bigr]\, \mathsf{V}^{n}
   \;+\; \theta\, \mathbf{s}^{n+1}
   \;+\; (1-\theta)\, \mathbf{s}^{n},
\end{equation}
with the two Eigen \code{SparseMatrix<scalar,RowMajor>} operators
\begin{equation}
  \Aimp \;=\; \Mmass/\Delta t \,-\, \theta\, \Lop,
   \qquad
  \Bimp \;=\; \Mmass/\Delta t \,+\, (1-\theta)\, \Lop,
   \qquad
   \Lop \;=\; \Kstif/(\chi C_{m}).
\end{equation}
$\Aimp$ and $\Bimp$ are rebuilt at every time step. When $\theta = 1$
(BE) the explicit half of the diffusion vanishes and
$\Bimp = \Mmass/\Delta t$.

\paragraph{Old-time source $\mathbf{s}^{n}$.}
The vector $\mathbf{s}^{n}$ used in the RHS is \emph{not} carried over
verbatim from the previous step. At the start of each step the TNNP
ODE is integrated once from $\mathbf{s}^{n}$ with $\Vm^{n}$ frozen,
producing $\Iion^{n}$ and hence $\mathbf{s}^{n}$; this is the
\emph{step-entry} solve. Subsequent Picard sweeps (Section
\ref{sec:nonlinear}) only overwrite the new-time iterate
$\mathbf{s}(\Iion(\mathsf{x}_{k}))$ and leave the step-entry
$\mathbf{s}^{n}$ in place. Across time steps the consistency of
$\mathbf{s}^{n}$ relies on the ionic state vector
$\mathbf{s}_{\!j}^{n}$ accepted at the end of the previous step being
the same one used to recompute $\Iion^{n}$ here.

% ================================================================== %
\section{Spatial discretisation}
% ================================================================== %

\subsection{Standard orthogonal FV Laplacian}
\label{sec:stdFV}

\code{assembleStandardOrthogonalStiffnessMatrix} produces the classical
5/7-point Laplacian. Per internal face $f$,
\begin{equation}
  a_{f} \;=\;
   \frac{ \lvert \mathbf{S}_{f} \rvert\,
          \bigl( \mathbf{n}_{f} \cdot \Dten_{f} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{d}}_{f} \bigr) }
        { \lvert \mathbf{d}_{f} \rvert },
   \quad
   \Dten_{f} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,(\Dten_{\text{own}} + \Dten_{\text{nei}}),
\end{equation}
yielding the four standard contributions to $\Kstif$. \code{zeroGradient}
and \code{empty} patches contribute nothing; \code{fixedValue} patches
contribute only to the owner diagonal.

\subsection{High-order LRE diffusion}

When \code{useHighOrder\_Vm = true} and $d > 1$,
\code{assembleHighOrderStiffnessMatrix} integrates the flux on a face
quadrature with reconstructed gradients:
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_{f} \;=\;
   \sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}_{f}}
       w_{q}\, \lvert \mathbf{S}_{f} \rvert\,
       \mathbf{n}_{f} \cdot \Dten_{f} \cdot
       \Bigl(
         \sum_{j \in \mathcal{S}_{f}}
            \mathbf{g}_{f,q,j}\, \mathsf{V}_{j}
       \Bigr),
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{S}_{f}$ the LRE face stencil and
$\mathbf{g}_{f,q,j} = $~\code{QRGradFaceGPCoeffs}~\cite{cardiff2018s4f}.

\paragraph{Boundary faces in the high-order branch.}
The face-quadrature integration above is applied only to
\emph{internal} faces. Boundary faces are delegated to the standard-FV
path described in \S\ref{sec:stdFV}: \code{zeroGradient} and
\code{empty} patches contribute zero flux, and \code{fixedValue}
patches contribute the one-sided owner-only stencil to $\Kstif$. The
high-order LRE stencil is therefore strictly internal and the
formal spatial order is expected to drop to that of the standard
branch in the first layer of cells adjacent to a non-empty patch.
Provided the activation front is interior to the domain (the case in
the 2D benchmark used in Section~\ref{sec:results}) this near-boundary
order drop is invisible to the reported activation-time metric.

\subsection{Mass matrices}

Two options selectable via \code{massMatrix}:
\paragraph{Lumped.} \code{assembleDiagonalMassMatrix} writes
$\Mmass = I$.\footnote{The identity is \emph{not} the FE-style row-sum
lumped mass. It is the cell-averaged FV mass: the semi-discrete
equation has already been divided by the cell volume $|\Omega_{i}|$,
so the cell-average operator reduces to a scalar identity. The cell
volumes are absorbed into $\Kstif$ (via the face-area factor
$|\mathbf{S}_{f}|$) and into the source assembly. A reader coming from
FE codes should not expect $\Mmass[i,i]$ to scale with $|\Omega_{i}|$
here.}

\paragraph{Consistent high-order.}
\code{assembleConsistentMassMatrixHO} fills
\begin{equation}
  \Mmass[i, j] \;=\;
   \sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}_{i}}
       \frac{w_{q}}{\sum_{q'} w_{q'}}\,
       \phi_{j}(\mathbf{x}_{q}),
\end{equation}
where $\phi_{j}$ is the LRE Taylor-basis function of stencil cell $j$
(value $\delta_{ij}$ + grad + Hessian + 3rd-order derivative).

\subsection{High-order reconstruction at ionic quadrature points}

If \code{useHighOrder\_Iion = true} the ionic ODE is integrated at cell
quadrature points; $\Vm$ is reconstructed there by the same Taylor
expansion (\code{reconstructVmAtIionIntegrationPoints}). The ionic
contribution to the cell average is then
\begin{equation}
  \Iionsub{i} \;=\;
   \frac{\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}_{i}} w_{q}\, \Iionsub{q}}
        {\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}_{i}} w_{q}}
   \qquad
   \bigl(\text{\code{averageIionIntegrationPointsToCells}}\bigr).
\end{equation}

% ================================================================== %
\section{Nonlinear solver}
\label{sec:nonlinear}
% ================================================================== %

The fully discrete equation is nonlinear in $\mathsf{V}^{n+1}$ through
$\Iion(\mathsf{V}^{n+1}, \mathbf{s}^{n+1})$. The implemented closure is
fixed-point Picard. The solver does not currently read a
\code{nonlinearMethod} key and does not assemble a finite-difference
diagonal approximation of $\partial\Iion/\partial\Vm$; the
\code{nonlinearIterations} entry only sets the number of Picard sweeps.

\subsection{Picard iteration}

Given an iterate $\mathsf{x}_{k}$, the next iterate is the unique
solution of the linear system
\begin{equation}
  \Aimp\, \mathsf{x}_{k+1}^{\ast}
   \;=\;
   \Bimp\, \mathsf{V}^{n}
   \;+\;
   \theta\, \mathbf{s}\!\bigl(\Iion(\mathsf{x}_{k})\bigr)
   \;+\;
   (1-\theta)\, \mathbf{s}^{n},
\end{equation}
followed by the assignment
$\mathsf{x}_{k+1} = \mathsf{x}_{k+1}^{\ast}$. The ionic ODE is
re-integrated from $t^{n}$ to $t^{n+1}$ at every sweep using
$\mathsf{x}_{k}$ as the (frozen) input voltage.

\subsection{Iteration budget and acceptance}

The outer loop performs \code{max(nonlinearIterations, 1)} Picard
sweeps per time step. There are no residual-based nonlinear stopping
criteria in the current implementation: after the last sweep, the last
voltage iterate, ionic current, and ionic states are accepted.

\paragraph{Consequence of \code{nonlinearIterations} $= 1$.}
All runs reported in Section~\ref{sec:results} use one Picard sweep
per step. With $k=1$ the new-time source $\theta\,\mathbf{s}(\Iion
(\mathsf{x}_{1}))$ is evaluated from the iterate
$\mathsf{x}_{1} = \mathsf{V}^{n}$ (the loop entry value), and no
correction is applied before the voltage is accepted. The scheme is
then \emph{linearly implicit in $\Vm$ and explicitly lagged in
$\Iion$}: diffusion is treated implicitly via $\Aimp$, but the
reaction term is effectively first-order accurate in $\Delta t$,
\emph{independently of $\theta$}. The temporal error therefore tracks
the linearisation, not the formal order of the $\theta$-scheme; this
is the dominant source of the $\Delta t$-sensitivity observed in
Section~\ref{sec:results}. Restoring the $\theta = 1/2$ second-order
behaviour requires at least one residual-converged Picard sweep, i.e.
$k \ge 2$ in practice with a small stop tolerance on
$\|\mathsf{x}_{k+1} - \mathsf{x}_{k}\|$.

% ================================================================== %
\section{TNNP numerical protection}
\label{sec:tnnpProtect}
% ================================================================== %

The TNNP 2004 cell model contains several mathematical features that
become numerical traps when the state variables are driven outside
their physiological window by the outer solver: gate variables that
overshoot $[0,1]$ make the rate-balance equations stiff or unphysical;
intracellular concentrations that touch zero force $\log(\cdot)$ /
$\sqrt{\cdot}$ / $\mathrm{pow}(\cdot)$ to overflow; very large $|V|$
saturates the $\exp(\cdot)$ tails of the $\alpha/\beta$ rate constants;
and the $L$-type calcium current $i_{CaL}$ contains a removable
singularity at $V=0$ (a $V/(\exp(V/RT)-1)$ factor). The TNNP wrapper
provides a guard, \code{protectState(state, t, qp, phase)}, whose
job is to keep the state vector inside that physiological window at
the boundaries of every external call into the cell-model kernel. The
guard is intentionally minimal: it does \emph{not} fix the cell model,
it only prevents one bad time-step from cascading into NaN/Inf and
corrupting the rest of the simulation.

\subsection{Master switch and configuration}
\label{sec:tnnp:knobs}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Key & Type & Default \\
\midrule
\code{TNNPNumericalProtection} & Switch & \code{false} \\
\code{TNNPClampGates} & Switch & \code{true} \\
\code{TNNPClampVmForRates} & Switch & \code{true} \\
\code{TNNPLogNumericalProtection} & Switch & \code{true} \\
\code{TNNPConcentrationFloor} & scalar (mM) & $10^{-12}$ \\
\code{TNNPVMinForRates} & scalar (mV) & $-200$ \\
\code{TNNPVMaxForRates} & scalar (mV) & $+100$ \\
\code{TNNPVoltageZeroEps} & scalar (mV) & $10^{-8}$ \\
\code{TNNPMaxProtectionLogEntries} & label & $10^{6}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The master switch is \code{TNNPNumericalProtection}. When it is
\code{false}, \code{protectState} returns immediately and \emph{none}
of the sub-switches have any effect; in particular,
\code{TNNPClampGates} and \code{TNNPClampVmForRates} default to
\code{true} but are inert without the master switch. The default
configuration is therefore \emph{all protection off}; production runs
typically need to set \code{TNNPNumericalProtection true} explicitly.

\subsection{Phases at which the guard fires}
\label{sec:tnnp:phases}

The wrapper invokes the guard at four phases, distinguished by a
string tag passed to \code{protectState} (used only for logging /
accounting; see \S\ref{sec:tnnp:log}):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Phase tag & Call site & Acts on \\
\midrule
\code{calculateCurrent} & entry of \code{calculateCurrent}
   & the state vector passed in for the algebraic
     $\Iion$ evaluation \\
\code{solveODE\_start}  & entry of \code{solveODE}
   & the integrator's working state vector at $t^{n}$ \\
\code{solveODE\_end}    & exit of \code{solveODE}
   & the integrator's working state vector at $t^{n+1}$ \\
\code{derivatives}      & inside \code{derivatives()}, every
                          RKF45 sub-step
   & a \emph{copy} of the state vector (does not mutate $y$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

All four sites are gated by the single \code{TNNPNumericalProtection}
flag. The \code{derivatives} site has one important asymmetry: it
copies the integrator state and protects the copy, then feeds the copy
into the rate kernel \code{TNNPcomputeRates}. The integrator's working
vector $y$ is never modified mid-step, so the protection only changes
the value of the rates RKF45 sees, not the state it integrates.

\subsection{Corrections and their order}
\label{sec:tnnp:steps}

When fired, \code{protectState} applies the following corrections, in
this order, to the (possibly copied) state vector:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Non-finite $V$:} if \code{state[V]} is \code{NaN} or
\code{Inf}, it is reset to $-86.2$~mV (TNNP resting potential, by
construction inside $[\text{vmMin}, \text{vmMax}]$).
\item \textbf{Voltage clamp for rates} (if \code{TNNPClampVmForRates}):
$V$ is clamped to $[\code{TNNPVMinForRates},\code{TNNPVMaxForRates}]$.
After step~1 this step is a no-op; it only fires when $V$ was finite
but outside the rate window.
\item \textbf{Avoid $i_{CaL}$ singularity:} if $|V| <
\code{TNNPVoltageZeroEps}$, $V$ is pushed to $\pm\code{TNNPVoltageZeroEps}$
(sign preserved). This removes the $V/(\exp(V/RT)-1)$ removable
singularity in $i_{CaL}$ at the symbolic level. Steps~1--2 may have
moved $V$ off zero already; this is the safety net for the case where
$V$ is finite, inside the rate window, but right on zero.
\item \textbf{Concentration floor:} each of
$K_{i}, Na_{i}, Ca_{i}, Ca_{SR}$ is replaced by
\code{TNNPConcentrationFloor} ($10^{-12}$ mM) if it is non-finite or
below the floor. This prevents $\log(\cdot)$ and rational-exponent
operations on near-zero concentrations from overflowing.
\item \textbf{Gate clamp} (if \code{TNNPClampGates}): the twelve gating
variables $Xr_{1}, Xr_{2}, Xs, m, h, j, d, f, fCa, s, r, g$ are
clamped to $[0, 1]$ (with non-finite values pulled to $0$). This is
the most frequent correction in practice because adaptive ODE
sub-stepping can overshoot the bounds by $\mathcal{O}(\code{relTol})$
between $t^{n}$ and $t^{n+1}$.
\end{enumerate}

Each step is idempotent and operates on the already-corrected state,
so a single \code{protectState} call is enough — there is no inner
fixed-point iteration. The ionic currents are computed from the
corrected state after step~5.

\subsection{Interaction with the RKF45 step controller}
\label{sec:tnnp:rkf45}

The \code{derivatives} call site introduces \emph{non-smoothness} in
the right-hand side seen by RKF45: clamping is $C^{0}$ at best
(piecewise linear in the offending component), and the gate clamp is
a saturation that flattens the Jacobian once the gate touches its
bound. The RKF45 embedded error estimator implicitly assumes
$C^{q+1}$ smoothness of the RHS over the sub-step, so firing the
guard inside \code{derivatives()} can inflate the local error
estimate around clamp boundaries and trigger spurious sub-stepping.
Because the integrator's working state $y$ is \emph{not} mutated
(\S\ref{sec:tnnp:phases}), the practical effect is bounded: only the
rates are perturbed, the state recovers as soon as the active gate
leaves the clamp. Even so, a high count of \code{phase=derivatives}
corrections in the summary file is a diagnostic signal that the cell
ODE is being driven outside its valid regime — usually by a too-large
$\Delta t$ or a transient in $\Vm$ that the diffusion solver could not
follow. The recommended response is to investigate the upstream
cause; the \code{derivatives}-phase protection is a safety net, not a
correction mechanism.

\subsection{What the guard does not protect}
\label{sec:tnnp:notProtected}

The five corrections above address NaN/Inf propagation, gate excursion
outside $[0,1]$, the $V=0$ singularity of $i_{CaL}$, and concentration
underflow. They do \emph{not} address the following remaining failure
modes intrinsic to the TNNP cell model:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Removable singularities in the $\alpha/\beta$ rate expressions
of $i_{Na}$ and $i_{Kr}$ (notably the $1/(\exp(\cdot)-1)$ factors at
$V = -47.13$~mV and similar). These can be regularised
\emph{analytically} by L'H\^opital substitution near the singular
point; the present wrapper leaves them to the adaptive integrator.
\item Stiff blow-ups in the calcium-handling subsystem when $Ca_{SR}$
approaches the floor or when $fCa$ saturates close to $1$. Clamping
$Ca_{SR}$ avoids overflow but does not regularise the
$Ca_{i}/Ca_{SR}$ ratio that drives the SR-release flux.
\item Exponential overflow of the $\alpha/\beta$ tails for $|V| \gg
\text{vmMax}$. Clamping $V$ to the rate window is a workaround; the
true fix is to ensure $V$ never leaves the physiological window in
the first place (i.e. solve the PDE accurately).
\end{itemize}

In short: the guard turns a numerical disaster (NaN cascade) into a
recoverable mis-evaluation. It is not a substitute for an
adequately-resolved outer solver.

\subsection{Logging and accounting}
\label{sec:tnnp:log}

Each invocation of \code{correctStateComponent} records a triplet
\code{(phase, variable, reason)} together with the offending value,
its corrected value, and the simulation time. If
\code{TNNPLogNumericalProtection} is true, the entries are accumulated
in memory (capped by \code{TNNPMaxProtectionLogEntries}) and emitted
on solver exit to the file

\smallskip
\code{TNNP\_numericalProtection\_summary.dat}
\smallskip

\noindent in the case directory. The summary groups corrections by
the \code{(phase, variable, reason)} key, with: count, first
occurrence time, the first old/corrected value pair, and the
min/max/$|\Delta|_{\max}$ over all corrections in the group. Typical
diagnostic use:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Many \code{Vm\_below\_minimum} at one specific cell ID $\Rightarrow$
suspect a Dirichlet BC pulling $V$ outside the rate window.
\item Many \code{concentration\_below\_floor} after a long simulation
$\Rightarrow$ ODE tolerances too loose; tighten \code{relTol}.
\item Many \code{phase=derivatives} entries during the upstroke
$\Rightarrow$ $\Delta t$ too large for the reaction stiffness; either
reduce $\Delta t$ or add Picard sweeps (\S\ref{sec:nonlinear}).
\item Any \code{nonfinite\_V} or \code{nonfinite\_gate}
$\Rightarrow$ a real divergence already happened upstream; the guard
contained the damage but the result is suspect.
\end{itemize}

\section{Activation tracking and benchmarks}
% ================================================================== %

\paragraph{Activation time per cell.} First-crossing of $V_{\text{thr}}$
in a step is recorded by linear interpolation:
\begin{equation}
  w \;=\; \mathrm{clip}\!\Bigl(
      \frac{V_{\text{thr}} - V^{n}}{V^{n+1} - V^{n}},
      \; 0, 1
    \Bigr),
   \qquad
   t_{\text{act}} \;=\; t^{n} + w\, \Delta t.
\end{equation}
The per-cell flag is cleared so re-crossings are ignored.

\paragraph{Early termination on P8.} When
\code{stopAfterPointActivation = true}, the cell nearest
\code{stopActivationPoint} (P8 in the Niederer benchmark) is monitored,
and the effective end time is set to
$t_{\text{act}} + $~\code{stopDelayAfterActivation}.

\paragraph{Diagonal sampling.} \code{sampleIDW} performs
inverse-distance-weighted interpolation at \code{nDiagonalSamples}
points between \code{diagonalStart} and \code{diagonalEnd} with
$k = $~\code{sampleNeighbours}, $p = $~\code{samplePower}.

\section{Solver flow chart}

The pseudo-code below summarises the per-time-step flow and shows the
\pyconst{run\_convergence.py constants} / \dictkey{dict keys} that
control each branch.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{High-level time-step loop of
         \texttt{highOrderElectroActivationFoamImplicit}.}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Read dictionaries.
\State Assemble Eigen \code{SparseMatrix}s: $\Mmass$
       (\dictkey{massMatrix}), $\Kstif$ (\dictkey{useHighOrder\_Vm}),
       $\Lop = \Kstif/(\chi C_{m})$.
\State $\mathsf{V}^{0} \gets -84$~mV (initial condition).
\While{ $t^{n} < $~\pyconst{END\_TIME} }
   \State $\Delta t \gets $~\pyconst{DT\_VALUES} (current sweep value).
   \State Build $\Aimp = \Mmass/\Delta t - \theta\,\Lop$,
          $\Bimp = \Mmass/\Delta t + (1-\theta)\,\Lop$
          (\dictkey{implicitScheme} $\to \theta$).
   \State Apply stimulus to $\Iext$ (\dictkey{stimulusProtocol}).
   \State $\mathsf{x}_{0} \gets \mathsf{V}^{n}$.
   \For{ $k = 0, 1, \dots, $~\pyconst{IMPLICIT\_NONLINEAR\_ITERATIONS}$-1$ }
      \State Reconstruct $\Vm$ at quadrature points if
             \dictkey{useHighOrder\_Iion}.
      \State Integrate TNNP ODE with $\mathsf{x}_{k}$
             (\dictkey{ODE\_REL\_TOL}, \dictkey{ODE\_ABS\_TOL},
              \dictkey{solver=RKF45}).
      \State Build RHS:
             $\mathsf{r}_{k} = \Bimp\,\mathsf{V}^{n}
                 + \theta\,\mathbf{s}(\Iion(\mathsf{x}_{k}))
                 + (1-\theta)\,\mathbf{s}^{n}$.
      \State Solve $\Aimp\,\mathsf{V}_{*} = \mathsf{r}_{k}$ with Eigen
             (\dictkey{linearSolver}, \dictkey{tolerance},
              \dictkey{maxIterations}).
      \State $\mathsf{x}_{k+1} \gets \mathsf{V}_{*}$.
   \EndFor
   \State Accept the last Picard iterate, ionic current, and ionic states.
   \State Record activation times (\dictkey{activationThreshold});
          P8 stop check (\dictkey{stopAfterPointActivation},
          \dictkey{stopDelayAfterActivation}).
   \State $t^{n+1} \gets t^{n} + \Delta t$.
\EndWhile
\State Post-process: IDW sampling at P8 and diagonal
       (\dictkey{nDiagonalSamples}, \dictkey{sampleNeighbours},
        \dictkey{samplePower}).
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\textbf{Legend.} \pyconst{Blue} = Python constant set in
\code{run\_convergence.py}; \dictkey{green} = OpenFOAM dictionary key
read by the solver.

\label{sec:results}
% ================================================================== %

This section collects measured P8 activation times, wall-clock cost
and peak memory.

\paragraph{Scope and disclaimer.}
The published Niederer et al.~2012~\cite{niederer2012} benchmark is
three-dimensional (20$\times$7$\times$3~mm anisotropic slab with
fibre rotation). The case used here is a 2D reduction
(20$\times$8~mm, single cell in $z$, planar fibres) defined for
code-development testing only and not reflected, to our knowledge, in
any published reference. The numbers below should therefore be read
as an \emph{internal} self-consistency study --- $h$- and
$\Delta t$-refinement, plus the effect of the LRE $p$-levels --- and
not as a comparison with any external reference value.

Rows of the tables below are LRE p-levels for $\Vm$ $\times$ $\Iion$
(\texttt{NO} = high-order disabled); columns are mesh sizes
$\Delta x$ in millimetres. Computational cost is the elapsed wall-clock
time of the solver process reported by \texttt{/usr/bin/time -v}; peak
memory is the maximum resident set size of the same process. The
time-integration scheme and mass matrix are noted above each block when
relevant. \textsc{NC} = no run data found for
that combination.

In the reported runs, \code{IMPLICIT\_NONLINEAR\_ITERATIONS=1}, so the
method behaves as a semi-implicit or linearly implicit scheme:
diffusion is treated implicitly, while the ionic source is evaluated
from a lagged Picard voltage and is not nonlinearly converged
(see \S\ref{sec:nonlinear} for the consequences).

\subsection{Discussion of the 2D results}
\label{sec:results:discussion}

The four scalar refinement axes --- mesh size $\Delta x$, time step
$\Delta t$, LRE order on the diffusion (\code{useHighOrder\_Vm}), and
LRE order on the ionic source (\code{useHighOrder\_Iion}) --- can be
read off the tables in pairs. The observations below use the
$P_{8}$ activation time $t_{\text{act}}^{P8}$ as the single scalar
output (in ms).

\paragraph{$h$-refinement at fixed $\Delta t$ (low order).}
For the pair (\code{NO}, \code{NO}) with Crank--Nicolson and
consistent mass matrix, holding $\Delta t = 10^{-3}$~s and refining
the mesh gives, from $\Delta x = 0.5$ to $0.05$~mm,
$t_{\text{act}}^{P8} = 192.75,\, 99.19,\, 92.01,\, 91.96,\, 91.51$~ms.
Only the two coarsest meshes are clearly under-resolved; from
$\Delta x \le 0.125$~mm the value is mesh-stable to within $\sim$0.5~ms.
At this $\Delta t$ the front is well-resolved in space even with the
low-order scheme.

\paragraph{$\Delta t$-refinement at fixed $h$ (low order).}
On the same low-order pair at $\Delta x = 0.05$~mm, refining
$\Delta t$ from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-6}$~s gives $91.51,\, 50.29,\,
46.10,\, 45.69$~ms. A one-order-of-magnitude reduction in $\Delta t$
between $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-4}$ \emph{halves} the activation time; the
following decade ($10^{-4} \to 10^{-5}$) moves it by another $\sim$4~ms,
and the last decade ($10^{-5} \to 10^{-6}$) by only $\sim$0.4~ms. This
is not the $\theta = 1/2$ asymptotic order of Crank--Nicolson; it is
the linearisation-error signature of \code{nonlinearIterations} $= 1$
(\S\ref{sec:nonlinear}). With a single Picard sweep, $\Delta t$ is
bounded above by the reaction stiffness rather than by the diffusion
stability or the formal $\theta$-order. Practical mesh-converged
behaviour for the low-order pair therefore requires
$\Delta t \lesssim 10^{-5}$~s in this benchmark.

\paragraph{Effect of the LRE $p$-level on the source.}
Varying only the second slot at the same $(\Delta x, \Delta t)$ ---
e.g. (\code{NO}, \code{NO}) vs.\ (\code{NO}, \code{p2}) --- isolates
the high-order ionic integration. At
$\Delta x = 0.5$~mm and $\Delta t = 10^{-5}$~s,
$t_{\text{act}}^{P8}$ moves from $157.69$ to $35.26$~ms; at
$\Delta x = 0.25$~mm from $67.69$ to $43.20$~ms. The cell-averaged
source $\overline{\Iion}$ computed at LRE cell quadrature points
differs from $\Iion(\overline{\Vm})$ wherever $\Vm$ has non-trivial
curvature across the cell --- exactly during the upstroke --- and the
difference is large on coarse meshes. The size of the
(\code{NO}, \code{NO})~vs.~(\code{NO}, \code{p2}) gap is a useful
proxy for under-resolution of the front at that $(\Delta x,\Delta t)$:
when the two configurations agree, the cell is small enough that
piecewise-constant $\Vm$ inside the cell is a good approximation.

\paragraph{Effect of the LRE $p$-level on the diffusion.}
With (\code{p3}, \code{p3}) at $\Delta x = 0.125$~mm,
$\Delta t = 10^{-6}$~s, $t_{\text{act}}^{P8} = 45.73$~ms; at
$\Delta x = 0.5$~mm with the same $\Delta t$, the value is $36.61$~ms.
Notice the trend is \emph{non-monotone} in $\Delta x$ for the
(\code{p3}, \code{p3}) pair: the coarsest meshes give the
\emph{lowest} activation times, with the values increasing and
plateauing as $\Delta x$ shrinks (cf.\ also (\code{NO}, \code{p2})
which behaves similarly). This is the signature of an LRE stencil
that, on a very coarse mesh, sees a substantial part of the front
within its support and effectively over-smooths the source; on a
finer mesh the stencil is local enough that the average becomes
faithful again. The values at $\Delta x = 0.1$~mm,
$\Delta t = 10^{-6}$~s lie in $[45.04, 45.80]$~ms across all four
high-order configurations we inspected --- the best mutually-consistent
estimate of $t_{\text{act}}^{P8}$ available in the current data set.

\paragraph{Cost.}
Peak RSS grows roughly linearly in the number of cells
$N_{c}$ (the Eigen sparse matrices dominate memory). Wall time grows
roughly as $N_{c}/\Delta t$ for fixed end-time, with the LRE
high-order branches paying a constant-factor overhead from the larger
stencil (mass-matrix assembly and the per-quadrature-point ionic ODE).
At $\Delta t = 10^{-3}$~s the cost ratio between
(\code{p3}, \code{p3}) and (\code{NO}, \code{NO}) at fixed $\Delta x$
is much larger than the accuracy ratio at that $\Delta t$ --- but the
balance flips at $\Delta t = 10^{-5}$~s, where (\code{p3}, \code{p3})
on a coarse mesh already matches the low-order configuration on the
finest mesh.

\subsection{Conclusions on the 2D study}
\label{sec:results:conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item The dominant numerical error in the reported configuration is
\emph{the linearisation error from a single Picard sweep}. This is
visible in the strong $\Delta t$-dependence of the low-order pair
(192~$\rightarrow$~46~ms across the $\Delta t$ sweep on the finest
mesh) and the much weaker $\Delta t$-dependence of the high-order
pairs. Until the Picard loop is closed (either by adding sweeps with
a residual stop, or by adding a $\partial\Iion/\partial\Vm$ Jacobian
diagonal correction), the formal $\theta = 1/2$ second-order behaviour
will not be recovered.
\item LRE high-order integration of the ionic source (\code{p2} /
\code{p3}) buys substantial accuracy on coarse meshes and partially
compensates for under-resolution of the activation front. It is more
cost-effective than $h$-refinement at the same target accuracy,
\emph{for the activation-time metric}.
\item The high-order configurations agree among themselves to within
$\lesssim 1$~ms at $\Delta x = 0.1$~mm and $\Delta t = 10^{-6}$~s,
which we take as the internal-consistency floor of the present data.
We do not claim this represents the ``true'' value of
$t_{\text{act}}^{P8}$ for this 2D problem --- there is, as noted,
no published reference to compare against.
\item The natural next step is to enable a second Picard sweep
(or, equivalently, a tolerance-based stop on the Picard residual) and
re-run the $\Delta t$ sweep. We expect the $\Delta t$-sensitivity
above $10^{-4}$~s to collapse and the $\theta = 1/2$ second-order
behaviour to become visible.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Discussion of the 3D results}

a large constant-factor overhead on top of that: at
$\Delta x = 0.5$~mm the (\code{p3}, \code{p3}) cost is already
$\sim 38\times$ the (\code{NO}, \code{NO}) cost on the same mesh.
Peak RSS scales close to linearly with $N_c$ for the low-order pair
(157, 334, 1666~MiB) and adds the LRE-stencil overhead for the
high-order pairs.

The 3D sweep is therefore consistent with the 2D study but much
sparser: it confirms that high-order on the source is the
cost-effective accuracy lever, but the dataset is too thin to draw
quantitative conclusions about the asymptotic $t_{\text{act}}^{P8}$
of this 3D problem.
