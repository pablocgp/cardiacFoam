This report updates the verification documentation for \code{testHighOrderHeatEquationExplicit} using the current \code{example5} results in \path{/home/pablo/OpenFOAM/pablo-v2312/run/tutorials_electro/testHighOrderHeatEquationExplicit/results/example5}. The report compares the standard second-order finite-volume operator (\code{hoT\_NO}) with LRE high-order reconstructions for $T$.
\end{abstract}
\section{Governing Equation}
The solver advances a transient scalar diffusion problem,
\begin{equation}
\rho C_p\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \nabla\cdot(\alpha\nabla T) + Q.
\end{equation}
The explicit cell update is
\begin{equation}
T_K^{n+1} = T_K^n + \frac{\Delta t}{\rho C_p}R_K^n.
\end{equation}
For \code{example5}, the implementation uses $R_K^n=L_h(T^n)_K-Q_K^n$, where $Q$ is the exact FDA-like manufactured ionic source reused as a scalar heat-equation source.
\section{Manufactured Example5}
The exact temperature is
\begin{equation}
T^\star(\mathbf{x},t)=A\sqrt{1+t}F(\mathbf{x}).
\end{equation}
For two-dimensional runs,
\begin{align}
F(x,y)&=\cos(\pi x)\cos(2\pi y), & G(x,y)&=1+xy^2.
\end{align}
The source is built from
\begin{equation}
Q^\star(\mathbf{x},t)=A I_{\mathrm{ion}}^\star(\mathbf{x},t),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
I_{\mathrm{ion}}^\star &= -\frac12\left(u_1^\star+u_3^\star-T^\star\right)(u_2^\star)^2\left(T^\star-u_3^\star\right)+\beta_T\left(T^\star-u_3^\star\right),\\
u_1^\star&=(1+t)G+T^\star, & u_2^\star&=\frac{1}{(1+t)\sqrt{G}}, & u_3^\star&=0.
\end{align}
These $u_i^\star$ are analytical helper functions only; unlike the FDA solver, this heat solver does not solve ODEs.
\section{Spatial Discretization}
When \code{useHighOrder\_T=false}, diffusion is computed with the standard OpenFOAM finite-volume Laplacian. When \code{useHighOrder\_T=true}, face fluxes are computed using LRE-reconstructed gradients,
\begin{equation}
\Phi_f\approx |S_f|\sum_q\omega_{fq}\mathbf{n}_f\cdot\alpha\nabla_hT(\mathbf{x}_{fq}),
\end{equation}
and assembled from \code{fvc::div(fluxT\_HO)}. The labels \code{p1}, \code{p2}, and \code{p3} denote first-, second-, and third-order Taylor terms. The label \code{NO} denotes the standard second-order finite-volume calculation.
\section{Boundary Conditions}
For the high-order flux, \code{zeroGradient} and \code{empty} patches are treated as no-flux boundaries by setting the high-order boundary flux to zero,
\begin{equation}
\int_{\partial K\cap\Gamma_N}\mathbf{n}\cdot\alpha\nabla T\,dS=0.
\end{equation}
Fixed-value patches keep their boundary contribution in the high-order reconstruction.
\section{Time Integration}
Time is advanced with forward Euler. There is no implicit linear system, no ODE solve, and no consistent mass matrix in this solver; the finite-volume update corresponds to a diagonal cell-volume mass. Large time steps can therefore produce divergent spatial fits, which appear as large negative fitted orders in the tables.
\section{Error Measures and Rates}
The solver writes cell-centred errors such as
\begin{equation}
L_2=\left(\frac{\sum_K |T_K-T_K^\star|^2 |K|}{\sum_K |K|}\right)^{1/2}
\end{equation}
and reconstructed quadrature errors $G_2$, computed by reconstructing $T_h$ at cell quadrature points and comparing with $T^\star$ there. The expected spatial orders are approximately $2$ for \code{NO} and \code{p1}, $3$ for \code{p2}, and $4$ for \code{p3}.

\section{Discussion}
The current \code{example5} dataset contains only two-dimensional runs. The smaller time steps show the intended second-order behaviour for the standard operator and approximately fourth-order behaviour for \code{p3} in $L_2$ and $G_2$. The larger time steps, especially $10^{-3}$ and in several cases $10^{-4}$, are dominated by explicit time-step instability or temporal error, so their fitted spatial rates should not be interpreted as asymptotic spatial convergence.
